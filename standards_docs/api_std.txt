Drilling Fluid Rheological Properties

Overview

Rheological properties describe how a drilling fluid flows under applied
forces. These properties are essential for efficient drilling
operations, cuttings transport, wellbore cleaning, pressure control, and
hydraulic performance.

Plastic Viscosity (PV)

Plastic Viscosity represents the internal resistance to flow caused
mainly by the concentration of solids in the drilling fluid. Formula: PV
= θ600 − θ300 Unit: cP

Interpretation: - High PV may indicate excessive solids. - Low PV
generally indicates lower frictional resistance.

Apparent Viscosity (AV)

Apparent Viscosity represents the overall viscosity of the drilling
fluid at 600 RPM. Formula: AV = θ600 / 2 Unit: cP

Yield Point (YP)

Yield Point measures the attractive forces between suspended particles
and indicates the fluid’s ability to carry drill cuttings. Formula: YP =
θ300 − PV Unit: lb/100 ft²

Interpretation: - High YP improves cuttings transport but may increase
pump pressure. - Low YP may reduce hole cleaning efficiency.

Yield Point / Plastic Viscosity Ratio (YP/PV or Transport Index)

Formula: TI = YP / PV

Higher values generally indicate better carrying capacity relative to
viscosity.

Flow Behavior Index (n)

The Flow Behavior Index describes whether the drilling fluid behaves
like a Newtonian or non-Newtonian fluid. Formula: n = 3.32 × log10(θ600
/ θ300)

Interpretation: - n = 1: Newtonian fluid. - n < 1: Shear-thinning
behavior (preferred for most drilling operations). - n > 1:
Shear-thickening behavior.

Consistency Index (K)

The Consistency Index measures the thickness of a non-Newtonian drilling
fluid. Formula: K = θ300 / (511^n)

Higher K values indicate a thicker fluid.

Importance of Rheology

Good rheological properties help: - Transport drill cuttings
efficiently. - Suspend cuttings when circulation stops. - Reduce
drilling problems. - Optimize hydraulic performance. - Maintain wellbore
stability. - Improve drilling efficiency.

Common Fann Viscometer Readings

θ600: Dial reading at 600 RPM θ300: Dial reading at 300 RPM

These readings are commonly used to calculate PV, AV, YP, n, and K.

Summary

Key properties: - Plastic Viscosity (PV) - Apparent Viscosity (AV) -
Yield Point (YP) - Transport Index (YP/PV) - Flow Behavior Index (n) -
Consistency Index (K)

Together, these properties evaluate drilling fluid performance, optimize
hole cleaning, and improve drilling efficiency and well control.
